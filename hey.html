Understanding Depression

Depression is more than occasional sadness. It can affect a person's emotions, thoughts, physical health, and everyday life. Recognizing possible warning signs can be an important first step toward support.

🌧️

Persistent sadness

Feeling sad, empty, hopeless, or irritable much of the time.

🌙

Sleep changes

Sleeping much more or less than usual, or struggling to rest.

🍃

Loss of interest

Losing interest or pleasure in activities that once felt enjoyable.

🫂

Social withdrawal

Pulling away from friends, family, or usual activities.

These signs can have different causes and do not by themselves establish a diagnosis. If symptoms persist, worsen, or interfere with daily life, consider speaking with a qualified health professional.

Everyday Mental Wellness

There is no guaranteed way to prevent depression, but supportive habits and early care can help protect mental well-being.

🏃

Stay active

Try a walk, dancing, stretching, or another activity that feels comfortable and enjoyable.

😴

Prioritize sleep

Build a regular sleep routine and create a calm environment for rest.

🤝

Connect with others

Talk with someone you trust. Meaningful connection can help you feel supported.

🌿

Manage stress

Take breaks, practice breathing exercises, and make time for activities that help you relax.

🥗

Care for your body

Eat regularly, stay hydrated, and seek medical advice when physical or emotional difficulties persist.

💬

Ask for support

Speaking with a counselor or other qualified professional can help you explore what you are experiencing.

You Don't Have to Face It Alone

If you have been feeling persistently sad, hopeless, or unable to manage everyday activities, consider reaching out to a qualified mental health professional or trusted health provider.

You can also speak with someone you trust and ask them to help you find appropriate support.

Need urgent help? If you may harm yourself or someone else, or cannot keep yourself safe, contact local emergency services or go to the nearest emergency department. If possible, stay with a trusted person while seeking help.
